Pros

Timeless — won't look dated in five years. Reads "trustworthy institution" to directors and estate lawyers. Closest to the existing main site, so cheapest path to consistency.

Cons

Almost no visual connection to the app a user actually downloads — the parchment-to-pink handoff at install is a brand break. Quietest of the three; risks beige forgettability if executed lazily.

Wren's fit read

Strongest for B2B trust surfaces (care homes, partners). Weakest at the app store doorstep.

Pros

Cons

Dark + death-adjacent product is a knife's edge: one wrong note and it reads funereal instead of heirloom. Hardest accessibility lift — light-on-dark strains older eyes in daylight. Furthest from the app.

Wren's fit read

Strongest as a B2B/partner identity or a premium tier face. Too heavy as the everyday consumer front door.

WREN'S RECOMMENDATION

B leads, A supports, C retires to the cellar

One brand can hold two registers but not three. Wren's call: App Mirror (B) becomes the system — it's the only direction where the ad, the website, and the product are one continuous memory. Warm Editorial's serif headlines get absorbed INTO B as the display type for hero moments, giving you warmth plus gravitas in a single system. Heritage Luxe is retired as a site style but kept as the print register for estate-channel documents. Disagree freely — that's what mockups are for.
